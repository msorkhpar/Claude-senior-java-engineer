## Determinism of the threaded practices

**Rules held in every threaded test:**

- A class-level `@Timeout(10 s, SEPARATE_THREAD)`.
- Daemon helper threads, and bounded `join` and `await` calls.
- Critical points are forced by test-supplied hooks, latches, barriers or a `Phaser`, never by `sleep`.
- "Blocked" is detected by `ThreadMXBean` lock owner or by a `wait` frame on the stack, never by polling `getState()` (see F2).
- Randomised backoff (the livelock fix on 6.2.1) takes a seeded `Random`.

**Before the pass,** every draft was proved in my runner:

- For each threaded practice: the reference 10 times, the starter once, and **every plant 10 times**.
- For other practices: the reference twice and every plant once.

**The 10x results on the 42 threaded practices:**

- References: 420 of 420 passed every case.
- Starters: 42 of 42 passed nothing.
- **Plants: 1040 of 1040 runs failed their own edge** (104 edges, 10 runs each) and passed every main case.

**The first 10x run found one plant passing 1 time in 10.** On 6.2.1 *listeners*, the test read its verdict after the bounded join, and the planted wrong solution had released the lock by then. The verdict is now taken inside the listener. An audit of every threaded test for the same late-read shape found none. It widened four bounds that relied on scheduling margins: 5.2.1 joinsum, 6.1.1 totals, 6.1.2 fanout, and 6.2.1 transfer. It also made 6.1.3 future's planted wrong solution unable to finish on any CPU. All five were re-proved 10x.

**Residual margins left as they are:**

- 5.3.3 lazy, and 5.3.4 registry and config, are 20 s.

## Findings

1. **F1, the Q2 reader is strict enough to exhaust the budget.**
   - Four questions were refused on Q2 in all three rounds.
   - Each time the reader named a different cue: a stem word echoed by one option, a nuance only the key answers, a hedge against absolutes, or option length.
   - Removing one cue exposed the next, so three quizzes shipped as shortfalls.
2. **F2, `getState()` polling is not a reliable blocked test.**
   - JVM-internal transient blocking let wrong solutions pass under load.
   - The fix: `ThreadMXBean` lock owner and `wait` frames. The skill's docs do not warn about this.
3. **F3, timing margins in threaded tests.**
   - Equal holder and probe bounds let a plant pass.
   - A verdict read after the bound let a plant pass 1 time in 10 (6.2.1 listeners).
   - Only the 10x plant runs exposed this; the pass's single live plant run cannot see it.
   - The skill's docs do not ask for repeated plant runs or warn about late reads.
4. **F4, a concurrency main case must not race.** Every plant has to pass every main case, and the docs do not say what that means for threaded practices.
5. **F5, a quiz shortfall leaves its section's examples unaccounted.** The pass then exits on the author's missing excuse, before writing anything. Readings have to be taken ahead of the pass so the author can write those excuses (the same as M9-6c F18).
6. **F6, the course repeats the same tasks across pages.** A cross-page dedupe was needed (10 drafts dropped).
7. **F7 (mine), I gave the wrong excuse-key form for a grader file.** My own instructions to the drafting agents said a bare path; the key is `tests:<path>`. The agents fixed it.
8. **F8, host load.** Parallel sections pushed the load average up to about 190, and maven runs took up to 90 s. No run was flaky.
